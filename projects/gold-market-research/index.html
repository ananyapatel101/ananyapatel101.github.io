Global Gold Market: Price Drivers and Investment Role

My MBA research project looked at why the gold price moves and whether gold earns its place in a portfolio. I traced how four macroeconomic forces (inflation, interest rates, exchange rates and economic uncertainty) feed into the price. Then I assessed gold as a safe-haven and investment asset, using secondary research on global demand, supply and market trends.

Research question

What moves gold, and what is it good for?

Gold pays no interest or dividend, yet central banks, funds and households keep buying it. The project set out to answer two questions:

  1. Price drivers. Which macroeconomic factors explain the long-run trend and short-run swings in the global gold price, and through what channel does each one work?
  2. Investment role. Does gold behave as a safe haven when other assets fall, and where does it fit alongside equities and bonds?

Framework

Four drivers, one price

Every driver was mapped to the channel it works through. Each one changes either the cost of holding gold or how much people want it.

Transmission

How each driver reaches the price

DriverWhen it…Usual effect on goldChannel
Inflationrises or is expected to riseUpPaper money loses purchasing power, and gold is seen as a store of value that keeps real worth over long periods.
Real interest ratesriseDownGold earns no yield, so higher real returns on bonds and deposits raise the cost of holding it.
US dollarstrengthensDownGold is priced in dollars. A stronger dollar makes it dearer for buyers using other currencies, which cuts demand.
Economic uncertaintyrises (recession, crisis, conflict)UpInvestors move to assets with no counterparty risk. This is the "flight to safety" effect.

These are the usual directions. They can break down in the short run, for example when a strong dollar and rising gold both reflect the same global fear. Separating the long-run relationship from short-run noise was part of the analysis.

For an Indian buyer there's a fifth factor: the rupee–dollar rate and import duty. A weaker rupee raises the domestic gold price even when the global price is flat.

Demand and supply

Investment role

Safe haven, hedge or diversifier?

I tested gold against three roles that are often mixed up:

RoleDefinitionWhat to look for
HedgeMoves opposite to (or independent of) another asset on averageRelationship with equities and inflation over long periods
Safe havenHolds or gains value during market stressGold's behaviour during crisis episodes and sharp equity sell-offs
DiversifierLow positive correlation that still lowers portfolio riskContribution to portfolio volatility when added in a small share

Trade-offs I weighed

  1. Gold has no cash flow, so it can't be valued with a DCF model. Its value depends entirely on what the next buyer will pay.
  2. It can go through long flat or falling stretches when real rates are high.
  3. Holding physical gold has storage, making-charge and purity costs. Gold ETFs and gold funds avoid most of these.

Method

How I did it

  1. ScopeSet the research questions and the four macro drivers to test.
  2. CollectCollected secondary data and literature on gold prices, global demand and supply, and macro indicators.
  3. OrganisePut the series in Excel on a common time basis so price and drivers could be compared period by period.
  4. AnalyseStudied price trends against each driver, noting where the usual relationship held and where it broke.
  5. EvaluateJudged gold's role as a hedge, safe haven and diversifier, and set out the risks.
  6. ReportWrote up the findings as an MBA research report.

Relevance

Why this matters for a finance role

  • Treasury and FP&A: the same drivers (rates, FX and inflation) move a company's borrowing cost, import bill and input prices.
  • Commodity exposure: a manufacturer that buys metals faces the same demand and supply logic when it budgets raw-material cost.
  • Evidence discipline: each conclusion is tied to a source, the same standard I apply to reconciliations and month-end work.
