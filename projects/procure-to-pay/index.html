The work covers the org objects, OBYC account determination, invoice tolerances and the payment program, and a 12-case unit-test script proves each control. The GR/IR balance it leaves at 30 September is the ₹3,40,000 in Part 5's trial balance.

Scenario

One purchase, five documents, three ledger entries

The Pune plant (IN11) of XYZ Pvt Ltd (IN10) needs 10 MT of 2 mm hot-rolled steel coil. The buyer orders it from Shakti Steel Suppliers (vendor 100001, Maharashtra) at ₹58,000 per MT, which comes to ₹5,80,000 before GST. It arrives in two lots, the vendor bills once, and the payment run settles the full amount (no TDS applies, see configuration step 11). All data is fictional.

The flow shows the combined goods receipts. In the ledger they are two documents, 5000000187 (6 MT, ₹3,48,000) and 5000000188 (4 MT, ₹2,32,000). Blue lines are debits and orange lines are credits.

Rule · MM–FI integration

  1. Assign org units before creating master data. A material can't be extended to plant IN11, and a PO can't be raised for it, until the plant belongs to IN10 and the purchasing org is assigned to both.
  2. OBYC before the first goods receipt. If valuation class 3000 has no BSX account, MIGO fails with "account determination not possible". I test each key in OMWB (simulation) before go-live.
  3. FSV before the first payment. Part 5's cash item covers 0000171000 (debit only) and 0000172000–0000179999, so 171100 would land in Not assigned and the Balance Sheet would stop balancing without any error message. Following rule INS3-01, I added the range 0000171100–0000171999 (D and C) in OB58 and re-ran the assignment check: 0 not assigned. 512000 already falls inside 0000511000–0000519999.

MIRO · OMR6 · MRBR

Three-way match: PO, goods receipt, invoice

MIRO compares the invoice with the PO (price) and the goods receipt (quantity). A breach doesn't reject the invoice. It posts, so the liability is in the books, but it gets payment block R, and F110 skips it until someone releases it in MRBR. Real errors, like a closed period or a duplicate reference, stop the posting altogether.

KeyChecksIN10 limitExample
PPPrice variance: (invoice price − PO price) × invoiced qtyUpper ₹5,000 or 2%, whichever is reached first. Lower ₹50,000 or 20%.₹58,900/MT: +1.55%, but ₹9,000, so blocked (P2P-08)
DQQuantity variance: (invoiced − received) × PO price₹0: any quantity billed but not received blocks6 MT received, 10 billed: ₹2,32,000, blocked (P2P-05)

A block is released in two ways. MRBR releases it by hand once the buyer confirms the price, as in P2P-09. It can also release automatically once the cause goes away: if the missing 4 MT of P2P-05 is received, the DQ variance drops to zero and the next MRBR run with "release automatically" clears the block.

F110 · FBZP

Payment run AP01

  1. ParametersRun date 20.10.2026, ID AP01, company code IN10, payment method T, next posting date 27.10.2026, vendor 100001.
  2. ProposalF110 selects open items that would be overdue by the next run: due date before 27.10.2026. Invoice 5100000093 is due 20.10.2026 (20.09 + 30 days), so it is selected.
  3. ReviewThe AP manager checks the proposal and the exception list. Blocked invoices (block R) and items not yet due appear there, not in the payment list.
  4. Payment runPosts KZ 1500000123: Dr vendor 100001, Cr 171100 ₹6,84,400, and clears the invoice.
  5. MediumCreates the bank transfer file for HDFC (DMEE). The bank statement later clears 171100 against 171000.

An invoice dated 30.09.2026 would be due 30.10.2026. That's after the next run on 27.10, so AP01 leaves it for a later run (P2P-11). This logic, paying what would otherwise be late by the next run, is what keeps suppliers paid on time without paying early.

  • GR/IR is an accrual the system makes on its own. The ₹3,40,000 of primer is already a liability because it was received. No FBS1 accrual is needed, which is why Part 5's six accruals are all non-PO costs: power, audit fees and interest.
  • Presentation. 219100 falls under Other current liabilities in FSV INS3. A debit GR/IR balance (invoiced but not received) should be shown as an asset. The GR/IR regrouping program F.19 (still used in S/4HANA) does this at period-end; at 30.09 the net balance is a credit, so nothing moves.
  • Old differences. Items that will never be settled, such as a short delivery the vendor won't send, are cleared with MR11 (GR/IR account maintenance), not with a manual journal.

Business impact

Why P2P controls matter

Purchases are a manufacturer's largest cash outflow: raw materials alone are ₹1.94 crore of IN10's ₹3.24 crore half-year revenue. The three-way match means the company pays only for goods it received, at the price it agreed. The duplicate check stops the same bill being paid twice. GR/IR books the liability the day the goods arrive, which makes the month-end close faster and more complete. And GST is worked out from the tax code, not by hand, which protects the input tax credit; checking TDS applicability up front avoids deducting tax the law doesn't require.
