Scenario

Enterprise structure for IN10

XYZ Group (company IN00) owns one Indian manufacturing entity, company code IN10 in Pune, which reports in INR under Indian GAAP (Schedule III). All data is fictional. The company code is the legal-entity level where the balance sheet and P&L are produced, and every global setting below is assigned at that level.

Rule · configure in dependency order

  1. Objects before assignments. A company code can't be assigned to a company (OX16) until both exist, and a variant can't be assigned (OB37, OBBP, OBC5) until it's defined.
  2. Number ranges before document types. OBA7 refers to a number range key. If FBN1 has no interval for that fiscal year, posting fails even though the document type looks correct.
  3. Periods before the first posting. OB52 is opened last, after the unit tests are written. Test UT-02 then proves that a closed period rejects a posting before the business relies on it.

OB52 · posting period variant IN10

Posting periods: two intervals, one control

Business impact

Why the foundation matters

Every later process (AP, AR, assets, the month-end close) depends on these settings, and they are expensive to change once documents exist. A fiscal year variant or number range set up wrongly shows up as a failed audit trail, not as a system error. Posting periods, field status and tolerance groups are also preventive controls. They stop back-dated entries, missing cost centers and oversized postings before they happen, which is cheaper than finding them later in reconciliation.
